# M&A Deal Protection and Transaction Completion

[Read the Full Research Paper (PDF)](Research_Paper.pdf)

## About the Project

As a student interested in corporate law and finance, I wanted to better understand how the terms negotiated in merger agreements relate to whether an acquisition is ultimately completed.

This project focuses on target termination fees, reverse termination fees, and material adverse effect (MAE) clauses. It explores both the legal and financial aspects of mergers and acquisitions through actual agreements filed with the SEC.

## Research Question

How do deal protection and risk-allocation provisions differ between completed and terminated M&A transactions?

## Data and Methodology

The study examines nine major public-target acquisitions announced in 2022. The research uses publicly available SEC filings, academic literature, and Delaware court decisions. Python is used to organize the data, calculate termination fee percentages, and conduct statistical comparisons.

## Key Findings

- Completed transactions had an average target termination fee of 2.43% of reported deal value, compared to 1.63% for the terminated transaction.
- Completed transactions also had higher average reverse termination fee percentages.
- Pandemic-related MAE exclusions appeared in all nine merger agreements.

Given the limited sample size, these findings are exploratory rather than evidence of a causal relationship.

## Future Research

I would like to expand this research by examining a larger sample of transactions across multiple years and exploring how different contractual provisions allocate financing and regulatory risks between buyers and sellers.

My goal with this project is to develop a stronger understanding of corporate law, financial research, and the role contractual negotiations play in mergers and acquisitions.

## TL;DR

Out of nine company acquisitions, eight succeeded and one fell apart. The successful deals had higher fees for backing out, suggesting that contract terms may play a role in whether a deal goes through. However, more research is needed to determine whether higher fees actually lead to more successful deals.
